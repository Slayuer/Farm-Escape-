# GAME DESIGN BRIEF

## 1. PROJECT OVERVIEW

**Working Title:** TBD  
**Genre:** Endless Runner / Arcade / Skill-Based  
**Primary Platform:** Mobile  
**Orientation:** Portrait  
**Visual Direction:** Cute, colorful, stylized 3D cartoon  
**Camera:** Third-person, slightly elevated behind the character  
**Movement:** Constant forward motion with free left/right steering

### Elevator Pitch

A cute vegetable escapes from a farm and makes a desperate run for freedom.

The player is constantly moving forward through crop rows, barns, fields, farmyards, equipment, animals, farmers, and increasingly ridiculous agricultural chaos.

The player steers, jumps, stomps enemies, destroys certain objects, collects coins, grabs power-ups, reaches farm stands, purchases upgrades, and attempts to travel farther than anyone else.

The game should be extremely easy to understand but difficult to master.

The emotional core is:

**"OH SHIT. I ALMOST HAD IT. AGAIN."**

Failure should usually feel like the player's mistake rather than the game cheating them.

---

# 2. CORE GAMEPLAY

The vegetable automatically moves forward.

The player primarily controls:

- Left/right steering
- Jump
- Aerial stomp

The player should be able to understand the controls almost immediately without a lengthy tutorial.

Movement should become increasingly satisfying as skill develops.

## Jump

Jumping allows the player to:

- Clear obstacles
- Avoid enemies
- Reach coins
- Reach power-ups
- Jump onto stompable objects
- Chain movement sequences

## Stomp

While airborne, the player can perform a downward stomp.

Certain enemies and objects can be destroyed by stomping.

A successful stomp may bounce the vegetable back upward.

This allows skilled players to create chains:

**Jump → Stomp → Bounce → Stomp → Bounce → Clear Obstacle → Land**

The stomp mechanic should provide substantial visual, audio, and tactile feedback.

# 3. WORLD

The game begins in relatively peaceful crop rows.

As the player progresses, the farm becomes increasingly chaotic.

Possible environments include:

- Vegetable rows
- Cornfields
- Orchards
- Irrigation areas
- Barns
- Farmyards
- Livestock areas
- Greenhouses
- Packing facilities
- Equipment yards
- County fairs
- Roads surrounding the farm

The environment should evolve during a run rather than feeling like one endlessly repeated field.

Possible obstacles include:

- Shovels
- Hoes
- Rakes
- Wheelbarrows
- Buckets
- Irrigation pipes
- Sprinklers
- Mud
- Fences
- Crates
- Tractor tires
- Tractors
- Harvesters
- Combines
- Conveyor belts
- Hay bales
- Farm gates
- Moving equipment

Some obstacles are avoided.

Some can be jumped.

# 5. CREATURES AND ENEMIES

Potential threats include:

- Chickens
- Roosters
- Pigs
- Goats
- Cows
- Dogs
- Rats
- Bugs
- Birds
- Farmers
- Farm workers

Enemies should have recognizable behaviors.

Some can be stomped.

Some must be avoided.

Some may chase the player temporarily.

Interactions should emphasize humor rather than violence or gore.

---

# 6. THREE-LIFE SYSTEM

Each run begins with:

**3 LIVES**

The player continues the same overall run after dying.

When the vegetable dies:

1. Remove one life.
2. Preserve coins collected during the run.
3. Respawn the player a short distance before the death location.
4. Continue the run.
5. Leave evidence of the previous death behind.

The respawn point should be far enough back that the player recognizes the approaching obstacle and gets another attempt.

After the third death, the run ends.

---

# 7. CORPSE COMEDY SYSTEM

When the player returns to the location where they previously died, their former vegetable should still be there.

Its condition depends upon what killed it.

Examples:

**Chicken:** chickens are pecking the corpse.

**Pig:** pig is eating it.

**Tractor:** vegetable is cartoonishly flattened.

**Farmer:** farmer is stomping or angrily attacking it.

**Sprinkler:** corpse is being blasted around in the mud.

**Goat:** goat is casually chewing on it.

**Combine:** ridiculous vegetable debris has been expelled behind the machine.

Deaths should be funny enough that failure itself provides entertainment.

Eventually there could be dozens or hundreds of death scenarios.

Players may intentionally experiment with hazards just to discover new deaths.

---

# 8. COINS

Coins are collected throughout the run.

Coins survive between all three lives.

Coins can be spent at farm stands.

Coin placement should occasionally encourage risky movement.

Examples:

- Coin trails near hazards
- Coins above stompable enemies
- Large coin groups requiring difficult jumps
- Rare valuable coins in dangerous positions

The player constantly chooses between:

**Play safe**

or

**Risk it for the reward**

---

# 9. FARM STANDS

Farm stands appear periodically during runs.

Their spacing should NOT feel perfectly predictable.

Seeing an approaching farm stand should create relief:

**"Just make it to the stand."**

The game transitions from frantic movement into a temporary safe zone.

Music becomes calmer.

The vegetable slows down.

The player receives a psychological break from the run.

The farm stand functions as:

- Upgrade shop
- Power-up shop
- Cosmetic preview area
- Premium store
- Progress checkpoint
- Moment of calm

When finished:

**BACK TO THE FIELD →**

The player exits the stand and immediately returns to the run.

---

# 10. RUN UPGRADES

At farm stands, players can spend collected coins.

Potential upgrades:

### Springy Roots
Increase jump height.

### Mud Stomp
Increase stomp radius.

### Lucky Harvest
Increase coin value.

### Thick Skin
Chance of surviving certain impacts.

### Crop Duster
Improve aerial steering.

### Horseshoe
Attract nearby coins.

### Hot Pepper
Successful stomps temporarily increase speed.

### Heavy Harvest
Increase ability to destroy stompable objects.

Not every upgrade should always appear.

Farm stands may present a randomized selection.

This forces players to adapt their build during each run.

---

# 11. POWER-UPS

Power-ups appear during gameplay.

Examples:

### Golden Carrot
Large coin reward.

### Hot Pepper
Temporary extreme speed.

### Potato Armor
Temporary protection.

### Coin Magnet
Pull nearby coins toward the player.

### Super Stomp
Temporarily destroy larger obstacles.

### Invincibility
Short period of chaos where the vegetable can smash through hazards.

Power-ups should be exciting because they are not constantly available.

---

# 12. DIFFICULTY

Difficulty should gradually increase.

Possible variables:

- Forward speed
- Obstacle density
- Enemy density
- Moving obstacles
- Narrower openings
- More complicated combinations
- Reduced reaction time
- Environmental hazards
- Enemy behaviors

Difficulty should primarily test player skill.

Avoid unavoidable deaths.

A skilled player should usually understand what they did wrong.

---

# 13. NEAR-MISS SYSTEM

Close calls should be detected and rewarded.

Examples:

- Barely passing a tractor
- Jumping immediately over an obstacle
- Passing extremely close to an animal
- Escaping a farmer at the last moment

Feedback could include:

**CLOSE CALL!**

plus:

- Small score bonus
- Sound effect
- Camera response
- Haptic feedback
- Temporary multiplier increase

Near misses should encourage controlled risk-taking.

---

# 14. SCORE MULTIPLIER

Successful play gradually increases a multiplier.

Example:

**x1 → x2 → x4 → x8 → x16**

Actions contributing toward multiplier growth might include:

- Distance
- Stomps
- Near misses
- Coin chains
- Perfect jumps
- Risky maneuvers

Taking damage may reduce or reset the multiplier.

This creates loss aversion as successful runs become increasingly valuable.

---

# 15. LEADERBOARDS

The game should eventually support:

- Global leaderboard
- Friends leaderboard
- Weekly leaderboard
- Challenge leaderboard

Primary endless-run ranking:

**Maximum Distance**

Player profiles could show:

**#37 — TaterThot — 18,492m**

and:

**Killed by an extremely angry goat.**

The cause of death should become part of the game's personality.

- Onion
- Carrot
- Corn
- Radish

- Chili Pepper

- Tomato

---

# 19. CHARACTER DAMAGE

The vegetable should visually deteriorate after each death.

### Life 1
Perfect adorable vegetable.

### Life 2
Bruised, dirty, damaged leaf, Band-Aid, etc.

### Life 3
Black eye, missing leaf, cracked skin, dirt-covered and visibly exhausted.

Despite this:

**THE VEGETABLE KEEPS RUNNING.**

This gives the three-life system visual personality.

---

# 20. GAME OVER

After the third death:

**THE FARM GOT YOU.**

Display statistics such as:

**Distance:** 4,821m  
**Coins:** 1,430  
**Stomps:** 87  
**Close Calls:** 34  
**Farm Stands:** 6  
**Cause of Death:** Angry Goat

If applicable:

**NEW RECORD!**

Then:

**TAP TO ESCAPE AGAIN**

Restart should be extremely fast.

Avoid unnecessary menus or loading screens.

---

# 21. MONETIZATION

Monetization should primarily occur at farm stands or outside active runs.

Do not constantly interrupt gameplay with purchase screens.

Possible premium content:

- Vegetable characters
- Character skins
- Hats
- Costumes
- Trails
- Stomp effects
- Death effects
- Farm stand themes
- Seasonal cosmetics
- Premium currency
- Harvest Pass / seasonal pass

A premium section of the farm stand can visually contain special merchandise.

Competitive leaderboards should NOT become pay-to-win.

Purchasing something should not allow a mediocre player to buy a top leaderboard position.

# 22. PSYCHOLOGICAL / GAME-DESIGN PRINCIPLES

The design intentionally draws from the following concepts.

## Near-Miss Effect

Players frequently narrowly avoid failure.

Near misses receive satisfying feedback.

## Hedonic Adaptation Restriction

Do not constantly overwhelm players with rewards.

Alternate between:

**calm → tension → reward → calm → chaos**

Farm stands are particularly important for this.

## Juiciness

Actions should feel disproportionately satisfying.

Use:

- Animation
- Sound
- Camera movement
- Particles
- Haptics
- Squash/stretch
- Character reactions

## Zeigarnik Effect

Players should frequently see an unfinished objective.

Examples:

- Farm stand visible ahead
- Next environment approaching
- Almost reaching a high score
- Weekly challenge progress
- Nearly completing an upgrade

## Intermittent Reinforcement

Not every reward should be predictable.

Rare power-ups, unusual encounters and randomized farm stands provide unexpected rewards.

## Skill-Acquisition Friction

Controls are simple.

Mastery is difficult.

Players gradually become dramatically better at movement, jumping, stomping and obstacle recognition.

## Frictionless Reset Loop

Failure should transition back into gameplay extremely quickly.

Avoid unnecessary menus.

## Cognitive Load Persistence

Players learn:

- Obstacle behaviors
- Enemy patterns
- Timing
- Stompable objects
- Dangerous combinations

Experience makes future runs better.

## Sympathetic Nervous System Activation

As runs become dangerous:

- Speed increases
- Music intensifies
- Obstacles become tighter
- Near misses increase
- Player's accumulated score becomes more valuable

Then farm stands temporarily release this tension.

## Kinaesthetic Flow

Eventually movement should become instinctive.

The player should feel like they are controlling the vegetable rather than consciously thinking about individual buttons.

## Loss Aversion

Long successful runs accumulate value.

Players become increasingly protective of:

- Distance
- Multiplier
- Coins
- Challenge progress
- High scores

The farther they travel, the more stressful mistakes become.

---

# 23. CORE DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

The game should create the repeating emotional sequence:

**SEE → REACT → DODGE → STOMP → NEAR MISS → REWARD → ACCELERATE → PANIC → FAIL → LAUGH → TRY AGAIN**

Failure should frequently be entertaining.

Success should feel earned.

Controls should be simple.

Mastery should be deep.

The player should constantly believe:

**"I can beat that next time."**

# 24. MVP — BUILD THIS FIRST

Do NOT attempt to build the entire final game immediately.

The first playable prototype should contain ONLY:

- One vegetable
- One farm environment
- Constant forward movement
- Free left/right steering
- Jump
- Stomp
- Basic physics
- 10 obstacle types
- 3 simple enemies
- Coins
- 3 lives
- Death
- Respawning before the death location
- Previous corpse remains visible
- One farm stand
- 5 upgrades
- Distance score
- Basic multiplier
- Game-over screen
- Instant restart

NO multiplayer.

NO payment processing.

NO account system.

NO weekly backend.

NO complex cosmetics.

NO battle pass.

NO production leaderboard.

First determine:

**IS RUNNING THROUGH THE FARM FUN?**

If the answer is no, improve movement and game feel before adding additional systems.

---

# 25. DEVELOPMENT PRIORITY

Development should occur approximately in this order:

**Phase 1 — Movement**

Make running, steering, jumping and stomping feel excellent.

**Phase 2 — Obstacles**

Create the obstacle spawning and interaction system.

**Phase 3 — Core Run**

Coins, distance, difficulty scaling, lives and death.

**Phase 4 — Comedy**

Corpse system, reactions and funny deaths.

**Phase 5 — Farm Stand**

Pause/break system and run upgrades.

**Phase 6 — Game Feel**

Animation, particles, sound, camera effects and haptics.

**Phase 7 — Content**

More environments, enemies, hazards and vegetables.

**Phase 8 — Online**

Accounts, leaderboards, cloud saves and weekly challenges.

**Phase 9 — Monetization**

In-app purchases, cosmetics and premium systems.

**Phase 10 — Production**

Optimization, analytics, testing, balancing and store release.

# 26. INSTRUCTIONS FOR CLAUDE CODE

Treat this document as the high-level source of truth for the game's design.

When implementing the game:

1. Build modular systems rather than one enormous script.
2. Keep important gameplay variables easily adjustable.
3. Avoid hard-coding balance values whenever practical.
4. Comment systems sufficiently for a beginner to understand their purpose.
5. Explain major architectural decisions in plain English.
6. Do not add major gameplay mechanics that are not described here without discussing them first.
7. Prioritize gameplay feel over graphical complexity.
8. Build and test one system at a time.
9. Preserve working functionality when adding new features.
10. Keep the project organized so AI-assisted development remains manageable.
11. Design systems with future mobile deployment in mind.
12. Keep leaderboard-critical gameplay fair and skill-based.
13. Separate gameplay systems from future monetization systems.
14. Optimize only when necessary; do not prematurely over-engineer.
15. When uncertain, implement the simplest version that allows us to test whether the mechanic is fun.

## MOST IMPORTANT DEVELOPMENT RULE

**Do not build the giant game yet.**

Build the smallest playable version of the core experience.

The first milestone is:

> A cute vegetable can run down a farm row, steer, jump, stomp, collect coins, avoid hazards, die hilariously, respawn and make the player immediately want another attempt.

If that experience is fun, expand from there.
